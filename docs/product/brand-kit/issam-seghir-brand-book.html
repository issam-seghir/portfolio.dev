The name: a word for the self-made

This is a personal brand, so the name is fixed: it's yours. What the brand does is choose which of its meanings to stand on. In Arabic, ʿIṣām is the man the word عصامي comes from, the everyday word for someone who made himself without inherited rank. For a self-taught engineer who founded his own products, the meaning is already the pitch.

The spelling. "Issam" doubles the s to keep the Arabic ص sharp and short-vowelled in French and English, the convention across the Maghreb. "Seghir" is the Algerian civil-register spelling of صغير, rather than Saghir or Sghir. Both match the GitHub handle issam-seghir, so the name is spelled one way everywhere.

In the classical sources

All quotations are from al-Maydānī's Majmaʿ al-Amthāl, proverb no. 4189 (vol. 2, p. 331), as printed in the al-Maktaba al-Shāmila edition.

نَفْسُ عِصَامٍ سَوَّدَتْ عِصَاماً

ʿIṣām's own spirit made ʿIṣām a chief.

The proverb itself. Al-Maydānī: «قيل: إنه عصام بن شهبر حاجبُ النعمان بن المنذر», said to be ʿIṣām ibn Shahbar, chamberlain to the king al-Nuʿmān ibn al-Mundhir.

كن عصامياً، ولاَ تكن عظامياً

Be ʿiṣāmī, self-made, and not ʿiẓāmī, someone who boasts of ancestors who are now only bones.

Quoted in the same entry: «وفي المثل».

يضرب في نَبَاهة الرجل من غير قديم … يعنى أنه خرج بنفسه من غير أولية كانت له

Said of a man who becomes distinguished without an old lineage: he rose by himself, with no inherited start.

Al-Maydānī's explanation of when the proverb is used.

فإنِّي لاَ ألُومُكَ في دُخُول … وَلَكِنْ مَا وَرَاءَكَ يا عِصَامُ؟

I don't blame you for barring the door; but what news is behind you, ʿIṣām?

Al-Nābigha al-Dhubyānī, to ʿIṣām when he kept the poet from the sick king's door. Quoted in the same entry. ʿIṣām was the keeper of a door, which is where the mark comes from.

نَفْسُ عِصَامٍ سَوَّدَتْ عِصَامَا … وَعَلَّمَتْهُ الكَرَّ وَالإقْدَامَا

ʿIṣām's spirit made ʿIṣām a chief, and taught him to charge and press forward.

Given by al-Maydānī with «وقيل» ("and it was said"), without a poet's name; later anthologies attribute it to al-Nābigha.

The Shāmila text prints this line with misplaced vowels (نَفَسُ … سَوْدَتْ عَصَامَا); they are normalised here. Check against a printed edition before using it in print.

Why it works

  • It's a person

    Clients hiring a freelancer hire a person, not a studio. Using your own name keeps the promise personal and accountable.

  • Self-made is in the word

    Arabic speakers already know عصامي. You taught yourself and founded your own products, so the name tells the story before the About page does.

  • The keeper of the door

    ʿIṣām ibn Shahbar kept a king's door. The portfolio is the door to your work, and the mark is drawn as that doorway.

  • To hold fast

    The root means to hold and protect, and the ʿiṣām is the strap that carries the water. Carrying a project safely to launch is the job.

  • The dot

    The saffron square is the dot of the i, a full stop and a text cursor. It marks things that are finished, like a shipped product.

  • Two scripts, one name

    It reads naturally in Arabic and in Latin letters, it's short, and it needs no "studio" or "labs" added to it.

The mark: a doorway with "is" inside

Everything is measured in the dot (d), the saffron square. The doorway is 10d wide; its straight jambs rise 7d to the spring line and the arch rises 6d more, so the opening is taller than a half-circle, the way a door should be. The letters are drawn with a 1d stroke and sit 2d from each jamb. Use "Show construction" in the header to see the measurements.
